Generative AI · Field Note 01

How a Large Language Model Is Trained

From raw text on the open web to a deployed assistant — the pipeline, the four resources that dominate the bill, and what it actually costs at frontier scale.

PREPARED BY Karthik Ram Senthilvel FORMAT Infographic SCOPE Training process & resource economics
01The Training Pipeline

Building an LLM is a sequential pipeline. Each stage feeds the next, but a single stage — pretraining — consumes the overwhelming majority of the compute, energy, and dollars.

01

Data Collection & Sourcing

Gather trillions of tokens from web crawls, books, code repositories, and licensed datasets. Llama 3.1 used 15T+ tokens.

input: raw corpora
02

Cleaning & Tokenization

Deduplicate, filter low-quality and unsafe text with classifiers, then break text into tokens the model can read. Data quality drives final capability more than raw volume.

quality > quantity
03

Architecture & Cluster Setup

Define the transformer (layers, attention heads, parameters) and provision a GPU cluster — often 10,000–25,000 accelerators wired with high-speed interconnect.

infra provisioning
04

Pretraining

The main event. The model predicts the next token across the entire corpus, repeatedly, for weeks to months. This single step is where ~90% of the cost lives.

the big compute event
05

Fine-Tuning & Alignment

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on curated examples, then reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to make the model helpful, honest, and steerable.

SFT + RLHF
06

Evaluation & Red-Teaming

Benchmark accuracy and adversarially stress-test for unsafe behavior, then add guardrails before release.

safety testing
07

Deployment & Serving

Optimize and quantize the model, stand up inference infrastructure, and monitor in production — an ongoing cost that compounds with usage.

output: live model
02The Four Resources That Dominate Cost

Training economics come down to four interlocking inputs. Push any one of them up and the others — especially the dollar bill — follow.

🗂️

Datasets

15T+
tokens (Llama 3.1)
Why it matters: diversity and cleanliness of data set the ceiling on capability. Curation and licensing are growing line items, not afterthoughts.
🖥️

Compute

16,384
H100 GPUs (Llama 3.1 405B)
Why it matters: GPUs are the single largest hardware cost. More chips train bigger models faster — at frontier scale this is tens of thousands of accelerators running in parallel.
⚡

Energy

11,390
tons CO₂e (Llama 3.1 405B)*
Why it matters: power draw is both an operating cost and a sustainability concern. *Meta reports net-zero market-based emissions via renewable matching.
⏱️

Time

30.84M
GPU-hours (Llama 3.1 405B)
Why it matters: wall-clock time runs weeks to months. Total GPU-hours — chips × hours — is the truest measure of training scale and translates almost directly into dollars.

The Bottom Line

Capability scales with resources — and so does the bill.

Every gain in a frontier model traces back to more and cleaner data, more GPUs, more energy, and more time. The cost has climbed from ~$4.6M for GPT-3 (2020) to $100M+ for GPT-4, with leaders forecasting $1B+ training runs ahead.

// PRETRAINING IS THE COST

One stage — pretraining — absorbs the vast majority of compute, energy, and dollars in the whole pipeline.

// GPU-HOURS = TRUTH

Chips × hours is the cleanest measure of scale and maps almost directly to cost, regardless of how a lab markets it.

// TRANSPARENCY VARIES

Open-weight releases (Meta) publish full numbers; closed labs disclose little. Comparisons are inherently part-estimate.
